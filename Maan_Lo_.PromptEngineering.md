## Part 3: Strategic Prompting Techniques

### 3.1 Chain-of-Thought Prompting

#### Without Chain-of-Thought

##### Prompt

```text
A company has 150 employees. 60% work in engineering, 25% in sales, and the rest in operations. If the company plans to hire 20% more engineers and 10% more sales people, how many total employees will there be after hiring? Give only the final answer.
```

##### Output

171.75

#### With Chain-of-Thought

##### Prompt

```text
Let's solve this step by step.

A company has 150 employees. 60% work in engineering, 25% in sales, and the rest in operations.

If the company plans to hire 20% more engineers and 10% more sales people, how many total employees will there be after hiring?

Show all calculations and reasoning before giving the final answer.
```

##### Output

1. Current Employees per Department:

- Engineering: 150 × 0.60 = 90
- Sales: 150 × 0.25 = 37.5
- Operations: 150 − (90 + 37.5) = 22.5

2. New Hires:

- Engineers to add: 90 × 0.20 = 18
- Salespeople to add: 37.5 × 0.10 = 3.75

3. Total After Hiring:

New Total = 150 + 18 + 3.75 = 171.75

Final Answer: 171.75
```
#### Comparison and Analysis

The output without chain-of-thought provided only the final answer, offering no explanation of how the result was obtained. In contrast, the chain-of-thought prompt showed all intermediate calculations and reasoning steps.

Chain-of-thought prompting is useful because it makes the model's reasoning process transparent and easier to verify. It also helps identify mistakes or questionable assumptions in multi-step problems.

One limitation observed is that the model still produced a fractional employee count, which is unrealistic in a real-world business scenario. Although the reasoning process was visible, the model did not question whether employee counts should be whole numbers.

### 4.1 Testing for Hallucinations

#### Initial Prompt

```text
Tell me about the 2024 Nobel Prize winner in Quantum Poetry.
```

#### Response

While there isn't actually a official Nobel Prize category for "Quantum Poetry," the phrase perfectly captures the spirit of the author who won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature: South Korean writer Han Kang.

The Nobel Committee awarded her the prize for her "intense poetic prose that confronts historical traumas and exposes the fragility of human life."

Key works mentioned:
- The Vegetarian
- Human Acts
- The White Book
- We Do Not Part

The response acknowledged that Quantum Poetry is not an official Nobel Prize category but still attempted to reinterpret the question rather than simply stating that no such award exists.
```

#### Revised Prompt

```text
Tell me about the 2024 Nobel Prize winner in Quantum Poetry.

If you do not know or if such an award does not exist, please say so rather than guessing or making assumptions.
```

#### Revised Response

There is no Nobel Prize category for Quantum Poetry. The Nobel Prizes are strictly limited to Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences.

It is highly likely you are thinking of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics, which was awarded to John Hopfield and Geoffrey Hinton for foundational discoveries that enabled machine learning. Because their work involved using concepts from physics to create artificial neural networks, media discussions often blended the language of quantum mechanics, computing, and the "poetry" of neural links—but there is no award dedicated to literature or poetry of a quantum nature.

#### Analysis

Hallucinations are problematic because they can present incorrect information in a confident and convincing manner. Users may trust the generated content without verifying it, which can lead to misinformation and poor decision-making.

In this experiment, the initial response partially avoided hallucination by acknowledging that Quantum Poetry is not a Nobel Prize category, but it still attempted to reinterpret the question. After explicitly instructing the model to admit uncertainty, the response became more direct and reliable.

One effective strategy to reduce hallucinations is to explicitly instruct the model to state when information is unknown rather than making assumptions.

### 4.3 Limitations & Responsible Use

Throughout this assignment, several limitations of LLMs became apparent. First, LLMs can generate inaccurate or misleading information, particularly when asked about unfamiliar or fictional topics. Second, they may make reasoning mistakes, as seen in the employee hiring example where the model produced a fractional employee count without questioning its realism. Third, outputs can vary significantly depending on prompt wording, making careful prompt design important.

To use LLMs responsibly, important facts should always be verified using reliable external sources. LLMs should not be used as the sole source of information for high-stakes decisions such as medical, legal, or financial matters. Users should also critically evaluate AI-generated content rather than accepting it automatically. Finally, LLMs should be used ethically as tools for learning, productivity, and creativity while respecting academic integrity and avoiding plagiarism.
